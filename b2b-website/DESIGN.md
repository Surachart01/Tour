# Vera Thailandia B2B Website Design System

## Direction

The site presents Vera Thailandia as an established, operations-led destination management company for international travel professionals. The visual direction combines true-black luxury hospitality, vivid orange wayfinding, editorial travel photography, and a quiet B2B interface. It should feel locally grounded, precise, exclusive, and easy for an agency partner to act on.

## Principles

- Lead with the real destination and the DMC offer in the first viewport.
- Keep operational information concise, scannable, and trade-focused.
- Use photography to show actual places and travel contexts, not generic decoration.
- Keep calls to action explicit: request a proposal, adapt a journey, or open the agent portal.
- Treat English as the default language and retain Italian as a first-class option.

## Visual Tokens

- Canvas: `#070707`
- Surface: `#101010`
- Raised surface: `#171310`
- Soft surface: `#211812`
- Primary text: `#f6f1e9`
- Secondary text: `#b9afa5`
- Signature orange: `#ff8a1f`
- Deep orange: `#d95f0e`
- Hairline: `rgba(255, 181, 112, .15)`
- Radius: 6px for controls and framed content, 8px maximum for cards

## Typography

- Interface and body: Manrope
- Editorial display headings: Newsreader
- Body text remains compact and readable; display type is reserved for hero and major section headings.
- Letter spacing is always zero.

## Layout

- Maximum content width: 1240px
- Desktop sections use generous vertical spacing and aligned content columns.
- Mobile sections collapse to one column; destination items become a horizontal, snap-scrolling list.
- The hero uses a full-bleed destination image with a single-color readability overlay.
- Fixed-format controls and buttons maintain stable dimensions across states.

## Components

- Buttons use Lucide icons where an icon improves recognition.
- Primary actions and operational states use signature orange; secondary actions use raised charcoal surfaces.
- Service rows are unframed list items for fast scanning.
- Journey cards use destination images with consistent aspect and text placement.
- Forms use visible labels, native validation, and restrained focus states.

## Interaction

- Header navigation is sticky and becomes a compact menu on smaller screens.
- EN/IT uses a segmented language control.
- The program planner creates a reusable brief and moves it into the request form.
- The request form prepares a structured email to the Bangkok reservations team.
- Reduced-motion preferences disable smooth transitions and scrolling.

## Motion Thesis

- The focal motion represents the DMC journey: the Arrival-to-Departure route draws forward and each operational waypoint activates in sequence.
- The hero settles from a restrained cinematic scale while copy reveals in a short editorial cadence.
- Section headings reveal once on entry; service rows, journey cards, and destinations follow with compact staggered timing.
- Hover movement is limited to directional feedback: arrows advance, imagery gains slight depth, and service rows expose an orange tracer.
- All content remains visible without JavaScript and every authored motion is neutralized when reduced motion is preferred.

## Accessibility

- Text and controls maintain strong contrast against their backgrounds.
- Navigation and form controls retain visible keyboard focus.
- Images include concise alternative text.
- Mobile touch targets are at least 40px high where possible.
